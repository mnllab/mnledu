🍽 What should I eat?

🎯 Tell me about right now

A daily question that never gets easier

The same exchange every lunchtime. What are we eating? Anything. Fine, the place from yesterday then. The trouble is rarely too few options — it is having too many to choose between.

This tool narrows things by asking a few questions instead. The weather, who you are with, what you are doing afterwards — answer those and 224 dishes come down to seven.

What it asks

☀️ Weather
Cold days call for broth, hot days for something cool. Rain brings pancakes and knife-cut noodles forward, the way it does in Korea.
👥 Who with
The heaviest factor. Eating alone points to dishes made for one; a formal table points to something properly turned out.
⏰ When
Mornings favour what sits lightly; late hours bring out the dishes people eat over drinks.
🩺 How you feel
Worn out leans hearty, stressed leans spicy, hungover leans towards broths and porridges.
🥗 In the mood for
If you already know what you want, say so here. Because you chose it directly, it outweighs the weather.
📅 After the meal
The same dish suits some afternoons and not others. Work ahead reorders things differently from an evening off.

How it decides

Every dish carries a rating for how heavy it is, how easily it digests, how spicy it is, what it costs and how likely it is to make you drowsy. Your answers combine those numbers into a score, and the highest come out on top.

The weights are ordered. Who you are eating with counts for the most, then how you feel and the weather, and what comes after the meal steers rather than decides. The nature of the occasion tends to settle the menu more than anything else does.

No more than two dishes from any one category make the list, so you will never get seven Korean stews in a row.

How to use it

  1. On days you would rather not decide, press Just pick something for me.
  2. Otherwise answer only the questions that apply — you do not need all of them.
  3. Show me gives seven dishes, with a note on why they came up.
  4. Not convinced? Shuffle gives a different set under the same conditions.
  5. Once you have decided, Copy puts it in a form you can paste into a group chat.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to choose anything?
No. Every option starts on no preference, so you can press the button straight away. On days when you would rather not decide at all, the shuffle button at the top hands you a single dish.
Which condition affects the result most?
Who you are eating with carries the most weight, because the nature of the occasion usually settles the menu. How you feel and the weather come next, and what happens after the meal steers the ordering rather than deciding it.
Why does it ask about after the meal?
Because the same dish suits some afternoons and not others. Heading back to work brings the lighter dishes forward; heading home lets the heavier ones through; a long drive pushes the spicy and the greasy down the list.
What if I do not like the suggestions?
Press shuffle again below the results for a different set under the same conditions. Changing one or two answers also works, and reset clears everything to start over.
How many dishes are in there?
224, spanning Korean, Japanese, Chinese, Western, Asian, street food, late-night dishes and cafe fare. Each carries a rating for how heavy it is, how easily it digests, how spicy it is, what it costs and how likely it is to make you drowsy.

Everything runs in your browser and nothing you select is sent anywhere. The suggestions rest on the general character of each dish — if you have an allergy or are watching what you eat, please judge for your own circumstances. Korean dish names carry a short gloss where the name alone would not tell you much.
