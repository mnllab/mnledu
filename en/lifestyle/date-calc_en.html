Date & Age Calculator

Adding, subtracting and counting dates in one place

Age accounts for whether the birthday has passed this year. Length of service is broken down the same way into years, months and days.

Where date arithmetic loses a day

A condition reading "within 30 days of the application date" leaves a real question: does the application date count as day one, or does counting start the next day? Contracts and legislation distinguish between the two, and the deadline shifts by a day depending on which applies.

Age has the same problem in reverse. There are several conventions, and different documents ask for different ones. This tool lets you pick the basis rather than assuming one.

Settings

Include the start day
Whether the first day counts. Legal periods often exclude it, but call documents vary, so follow the wording in front of you.
Business days only
Counts weekdays and skips weekends. Public holidays are not included, since they differ by country.
Age conventions
International age is shown alongside two Korean conventions, marked KR: Korean age, which starts at one and increments at new year, and year age, which is simply the current year minus the birth year.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include the start day or not
Follow the wording of the contract or notice. "Within 30 days of" usually starts counting the following day, but exceptions are common. Where an explicit deadline date is given, that takes precedence.
Are public holidays taken into account
No. Business day counting excludes weekends only, because holiday calendars differ by country. Check separately if the deadline depends on them.
What are the Korean age conventions
Korea historically used two systems alongside international age. Korean age starts at one at birth and increases every new year; year age is the current year minus the birth year. They are included because forms in Korea sometimes still ask for them.
