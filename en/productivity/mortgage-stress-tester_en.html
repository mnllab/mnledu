The monthly payment your bank quoted you is only valid at today's rate — once rates move, that number no longer holds. This tool exists to answer, right now: "if rates rise 1pp, 2pp, can my paycheck still cover it?" It calculates equal-payment and equal-principal repayments with exact financial formulas, and a rate stress slider instantly shows how a rate-hike scenario would change your DSR risk.

How is DSR calculated here

This tool's DSR is calculated as 'estimated monthly payment including the rate stress slider ÷ take-home monthly income × 100'. With the slider at 0pp, you see DSR at your current rate; move the slider up and you see DSR for that rate-hike scenario in real time. Since equal-principal repayment has its largest payment in the first month, we use that first-month figure as the basis — the more conservative choice, so risk isn't understated. A real institution's DSR can be calculated more strictly, often combining multiple loans, so treat this number as a reference.

Why the slider moves the DSR gauge too

A number saying "your payment goes up by this much" is hard to feel viscerally on its own. So moving the slider immediately changes the gauge's color and risk comment as well — nudging it up in 0.1pp steps lets you see exactly when your DSR crosses from safe (under 40%) into caution (40–50%) and danger (over 50%).

Why equal-payment and equal-principal calculate so differently

Equal-payment repayment uses an annuity-factor formula that adjusts the principal-to-interest ratio so the total you pay each month stays constant. Equal-principal repayment divides the principal evenly across the loan term and repays that fixed amount every month, while interest is charged only on whatever balance remains — so the first payment is heaviest and it gets lighter toward the end. Both formulas should produce identical results for a one-month loan, and this tool was specifically verified against that edge case.

Why the yearly schedule is shown as a table too

A chart shows the overall trend at a glance, but a specific number like "exactly how much balance is left at year 10" is clearer in a table. That's why a table with yearly principal paid, interest paid, and remaining balance sits right below the line-and-bar (balance/interest) chart.

How is DSR calculated here?
This tool shows DSR as the first month's payment divided by your take-home monthly income. A real financial institution's DSR calculation can be more complex, often combining multiple loans.
What does the rate stress simulator show?
It instantly recalculates your payment and DSR if you add the slider's chosen increase (in percentage points) to your current rate — meant to help you check your repayment capacity ahead of a rate-hike cycle.
What's the difference between equal-payment and equal-principal repayment?
With equal-payment (annuity), the amount you pay each month stays the same until maturity. With equal-principal, you repay the same principal amount every month while the interest shrinks along with the remaining balance, so the first payment is the largest and it decreases over time.
