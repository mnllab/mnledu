Whoever writes the roster takes the blame


You open a spreadsheet, fill in names one by one, and the same replies come back every time. Why am I always on the weekend? I had it last month too. How come they get off so lightly? You believe you were fair, but there is nothing to point at.


This tool builds the evidence alongside the roster. Enter the team and their days off, and once it runs you get a bar for each person showing how many weekdays and how many weekends they drew. Screenshot that and the argument is over.



Balancing the total is not the same as being fair


The usual approach hands the next turn to whoever has the fewest so far. Totals come out neat. The problem is that weekends end up lopsided.


Run a month for five people a few times that way and the totals sit at six or seven each, while one person picks up four weekends and another picks up none. The same number of shifts, a completely different burden.


So this tool counts weekdays and weekends separately. On a weekend it picks from whoever has the fewest weekends, breaking ties on total count. Run the same month again and the weekend gap usually drops to zero.



What goes in and what comes out


📅 Month
Pick it and the calendar draws immediately. Saturdays are blue and Sundays red, so the shape of the month reads at a glance.


👥 Team members
Separate with commas or line breaks. Enter a name twice and it is folded into one.


🏖️ Days off
Click a date and the team list opens. Tick whoever is away and a badge appears on that date; they drop out of the pool for that day.


🎌 Public holidays
Enter the dates and they behave like weekends. Holidays shift every year and differ by country, so you set them yourself.


⚙️ Rules
Choose whether to cover weekends at all, and whether to block back-to-back shifts.





You can still adjust it afterwards


No generated roster survives contact with real circumstances. Drag a name onto another date and the two people swap places. The statistics move with it, so you can check immediately that the balance still holds after the change.


If the whole thing sits badly, press Run again. Even under identical conditions the pick between equally-placed people is random, so a different arrangement comes out.

Frequently asked questions


Is the allocation actually fair?
Balancing the total alone lets one person collect all the weekends. So weekday and weekend turns are counted separately, and whoever has fewest of the relevant kind goes first. Run a month with five people and the weekend counts usually come out identical, with total counts within one of each other.


How do I enter days off?
Click a date on the calendar and the team list opens. Tick whoever is away that day; a badge appears on the date and that person drops out of the pool when the roster is built. You can select several people at once.


What if I do not like the result?
Drag a name onto another date and the two people swap places. The statistics update with it, so you can see straight away whether the balance still holds. Run again gives you a fresh allocation under the same conditions.


How does the back-to-back rule work?
Whoever was on duty yesterday drops out of today’s pool. On a day where the team is small enough that nobody else is available, the rule is relaxed for that day so the slot does not end up empty.


Are public holidays handled?
Enter the dates in the holidays field and they are treated like weekends. With weekday-only turned on those days stay empty; with it off they count towards the weekend tally so the balance still works out.


How do I share the finished roster?
Copy announcement puts a formatted list of dates, days and names on your clipboard, ready to paste into a group chat. You can also download it as a spreadsheet or save the whole calendar as an image.





Everything is calculated inside your browser. The names and days off you enter are never sent to any server. Closing the page clears the work, so copy or download anything you need to keep. The result is a proposal — real duty assignments should still take your organisation’s rules and each person’s circumstances into account.
