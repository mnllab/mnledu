The usual way to find a client’s brand colour


You are putting a deck together and it has to match the client’s brand. There is no brand guide to hand — just the logo sitting on their website.


So you save the logo, drop it into an image editor, and pick at it with the eyedropper one point at a time. Six or seven colours means doing that six or seven times.


This tool pulls them all out at once. Screenshot and paste is enough, and clicking a swatch copies its HEX code.



Three ways to load an image


📋 Paste
The quickest. Crop to the logo with your screenshot tool, copy, then press Ctrl+V on this page. No file to save.


🖱️ Drag and drop
Drag an image straight off your desktop or file browser.


📁 Browse
Click the upload area and pick a file.





How the colours are chosen


The pixels are sampled, similar shades are grouped together, and the groups are ranked by how much of the image they cover. The dominant colour is the largest group; the palette is what comes next.


Setting precision to Precise samples more densely and catches subtler shades, at the cost of a little time. For a logo with a handful of flat colours, Fast is plenty.


You can ask for anything from four to ten colours. An image that simply does not contain that many will return fewer, and you get however many are actually there.

The palette can also be saved as a single image, which is easy to attach to a document or share with a team.



When the result looks wrong


Shadows and lighting in a photograph shift the colours away from the source. For an accurate read on a logo, use an image with a plain background.


Cropping out shadows and borders before pasting makes a noticeable difference. A wide expanse of white background will sometimes come back as the dominant colour, which is technically correct and rarely what you wanted.



Frequently asked questions


Is my image uploaded anywhere?
No. The colour extraction runs inside your browser, so the tool works with the connection switched off. Close the page and the image goes with it.


Can I grab a logo straight from a website?
Take a screenshot and paste it in. Crop to the logo with your screenshot tool, copy, then press the paste shortcut on this page and the colours come straight out. No need to save a file first.


How are the colours chosen?
The pixels are sampled, similar shades grouped together, and the groups ranked by how much of the image they cover. The dominant colour is the largest of those; the palette is what follows. Raising the precision samples more closely at the cost of a little time.


Where do I use the copied code?
HEX works across CSS and most design tools. In presentation software you can usually paste the HEX straight into the colour dialog, and where a tool wants RGB the numbers are shown underneath each swatch.


The colours look off compared to the original
Shadows and lighting in a photograph shift the colours away from the source. For a logo, an image on a plain background gives a truer reading, and cropping out shadows or borders before pasting improves the result.


Can I take the whole palette at once?
Copy all puts the dominant colour and the palette on your clipboard as a single line of HEX codes. You can also export as CSS custom properties, a JSON file, or a palette image to drop into a document.





Everything runs inside your browser and the image you load is never transmitted to any server. The colours are calculated from the pixels, so they may differ from what a brand has formally specified. For exact brand colours, check the organisation’s brand guidelines, and consider separately whether you have the right to use their logo and colours.
