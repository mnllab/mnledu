Sometimes speaking is faster than typing


An idea arrives while you are walking. A meeting needs a note taken in real time. Your hands are busy. Dictation services handle all of these, but almost all of them send the audio to a server, which is a problem when the recording is a client conversation or an internal meeting.


This tool can download a speech model into your browser and run recognition on your device. After the one time install, your audio never leaves your machine. There is also a correction dictionary for the names and technical terms that recognition consistently gets wrong.


When to use it


Meeting notes
Capturing points as they come up, without the recording leaving your device.


Ideas while moving
Getting a thought down when typing is not practical.


First drafts of long writing
Talking through the material and shaping it afterwards. It gets past a blank page faster than typing does.


Domain-heavy vocabulary
Add the terms that keep coming out wrong to the dictionary and they are corrected automatically from then on.

Settings


Accuracy: strict, normal, loose
How cautious the matching is. Strict is more accurate and slower, loose is quicker with more mistakes.


Recognition language
English by default. Setting the right language noticeably improves accuracy.


On-device
Runs recognition locally once the model is installed, so no audio is sent anywhere.


Correction dictionary
Names and technical terms you use often. Stored in your browser, so it is still there next visit.




Frequently asked questions


Is my audio sent to a server
With the on-device model installed, no. The model is downloaded once and recognition happens in your browser. Without it, the browser's built-in recognition does route audio through a speech service, which is why the on-device option exists.


Why does the model take so long to install
It is a large file, so a few minutes is normal depending on your connection. Once downloaded it stays in your browser and starts instantly after that.


Which browser should I use
Chrome is the most reliable. Firefox does not support the API at all, Safari support is limited, and Brave blocks the speech server by default.


Recognition is poor. What can I do
Set accuracy to strict, get closer to the microphone and speak deliberately. If one particular word keeps failing, put it in the dictionary rather than fighting the recogniser.




This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server or stored anywhere, and closing the page discards the working data.
