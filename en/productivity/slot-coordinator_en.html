The "when are you free?" problem

A meeting request arrives and you open your calendar to scan for gaps. Factoring in travel either side takes longer than it should, and half the time you send options only to remember a clash afterwards.

Paste your calendar screen instead. It reads the entries, subtracts your buffers and lunch, and gives you the slots that are genuinely free — along with the message to send.

Where it helps

External meetings
When you need to offer a few workable times.
Scheduling across time zones
When the other side needs their local time spelled out.
Avoiding back-to-back days
When travel time between commitments matters.
Weeks with standing meetings
Recurring slots stay blocked without re-entering them.

How to use it

  1. Set your working hours, buffer and minimum meeting length.
  2. Paste a screenshot of your calendar — Ctrl+V works anywhere on the page.
  3. If anything reads wrong, drag on the grid to fix it.
  4. Pick the other party's region to show their local time.
  5. Press the message button for wherever you are sending it.

How screenshots are read

Weekly grid view
The time axis down the left and the day headers across the top give the coordinates, which are converted back into times. Entry colours are picked up too and shown faintly on the grid.
Agenda list view
Date headers with entries listed underneath. All-day items are skipped, since they do not block a specific time.
Multiple images
If the calendar is too tall for one capture, paste up to three. Overlapping entries are reconciled automatically.
Plain text
You can paste the schedule text directly instead of an image.

Frequently asked questions

What kinds of calendar screenshots can it read?
Both the weekly grid view and the agenda list view. It handles 9 AM and 09:00 as well as Korean forms such as 오전 9시. All-day entries are skipped, since they do not actually block any time.
My calendar is too tall to capture in one shot
Paste up to three images. Capture the screen in sections and drop them in one after another — entries accumulate, and anything that overlaps between shots is reconciled automatically.
What is the buffer for?
Travel or preparation time held clear on either side of a meeting. Set it to 30 minutes and a slot ending at 10:00 leaves you free from 10:30, so nothing gets booked back to back.
How are time zones handled?
Pick the other party's region and their local time appears alongside yours. Where a slot starts on one date and ends on another in their zone, both dates are spelled out so there is no ambiguity.
What if the screenshot is read incorrectly?
Drag across the timetable on the right to add an entry, and drag over an existing one to remove it. Recognition does not have to be perfect — you can correct it by hand in seconds.

This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Screenshots you paste and schedules you type are never transmitted. Text recognition depends on the quality of the capture, so glance over the grid before sending times to anyone.
