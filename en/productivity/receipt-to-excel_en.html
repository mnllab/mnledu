Typing receipts in, one line at a time


You tip the month’s receipts out of the drawer and open a spreadsheet. Date, merchant, amount, next one. Twenty receipts eats an hour, and somewhere in there you fat-finger a figure.


This tool takes over the transcribing. Drop a photo in and it reads the date, amount and merchant. For itemised receipts it treats OCR word coordinates as a table: item, unit price, quantity and total are assigned by column, and a second manufacturer/detail line is attached as the note. Up to ten line items are added from one photo. Fix anything that came out wrong, then paste the lot into your spreadsheet.



The photos never go anywhere


A receipt carries your company name and what you spent it on. Uploading that to somebody else’s server is not a comfortable trade for a bit of convenience.


Here, even the text recognition runs inside your browser. The recognition data downloads once, and after that no photo has any reason to leave the machine. Close the page and the work goes with it.



What it pulls out


📅 Date
Whether the receipt says 2025-08-14, 25/08/14 or 14 August 2025, it comes out as year-month-day. Impossible dates like a thirteenth month or 30 February are discarded and the next candidate is tried.


💰 Amount
Found near words like total, approved or amount due. If that line holds no figure it looks at the next one. Lines carrying approval numbers or registration numbers are skipped so they are not mistaken for money.


🏢 Merchant
A line explicitly labelled as the merchant comes first, then a company marker, and failing both it takes a plausible line from the top of the receipt, skipping registration and phone numbers.


📁 Category
Guessed from the merchant name and recognised text using a broad built-in keyword dictionary. Pharmacies and pharmaceutical companies map to Pharmacy, hospitals to Medical, hotels to Lodging, fuel and repair shops to Vehicle, software services to Software & subscriptions, and so on. It is still a heuristic, so ambiguous merchants should be checked.





Recognition is not perfect


Receipts print in every condition imaginable. Faded thermal paper, a fold across the middle, ink that gave up halfway — even a person squints at some of them.


So every cell in the table can be clicked and edited. The recognised text is shown on the left exactly as it came out, which makes it quick to see where things went astray. If any of the three fields is missing, you are told how many rows need attention when the batch finishes, and the summary row keeps a running count.


OCR depends heavily on photo quality. Keep the four receipt corners visible, fill the frame, avoid shadows and glare, and keep the print sharp. For itemised receipts, roughly 10 line items or fewer per photo is a practical target; photograph long receipts in overlapping upper and lower sections and continue scanning. The selected region is cropped from the original and resized to an OCR-friendly resolution before perspective and contrast correction. Curved paper, severe blur and blown highlights still need a better photo.

Frequently asked questions


Are my receipt photos uploaded anywhere?
No. Even the text recognition runs inside your browser; only the recognition data is fetched, once. The photo itself never leaves your device, and closing the page clears the work with it.


What if the reading is wrong?
Click any cell in the table and edit it directly. Receipts print in every imaginable condition, so perfect recognition is not realistic. This tool is here to save the typing; checking and correcting is quicker done by hand.


What does it pull out?
Three things: the date, the amount and the merchant. Dates come out as year-month-day whatever format they were printed in. Amounts are found near words like total or approved, while approval and registration numbers are filtered out. Merchants come from a labelled line or a company marker first, and from the top of the receipt otherwise.


Can I do several at once?
They are read one at a time and stack up in the table. Select several photos and they are worked through in order, with a new row appearing as each finishes. Export the whole lot when you are done.


How do I get it into a spreadsheet?
Copy for spreadsheet puts the table on your clipboard with tabs between the cells. Paste it into Excel or Sheets and every value lands in its own cell. To keep a file instead, use the CSV download.


How long does recognition take?
The first run spends a while downloading the recognition data. After that a receipt usually takes a few seconds to a dozen or so, depending on your device and the size of the photo.





Everything runs inside your browser and no receipt photo is transmitted to any server. Text recognition needs an internet connection to fetch its data file; even then, the photo itself is never sent. Automatically extracted values are a starting point — check them against the originals before you submit anything.
