A revision that does not say what changed


The other side sends the contract back, reviewed. Nothing marks what they touched. You put the two files up next to each other and work through clause by clause, and somewhere in there you miss the line that matters — the one where payment moved from thirty days to sixty.


This tool does that comparison for you. Paste the original and the revision and removals show in red, additions in green, with buttons to step from one change to the next.



How confidential drafts are handled


Uploading a contract or an internal document to somewhere on the internet is a problem in itself, because the terms under negotiation go with it.


Here the comparison runs inside your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere, so the tool works with the connection switched off. Close the page and what you pasted goes with it.



Choosing a comparison level


Word
The default. Good for seeing where a sentence or a clause was rewritten, since the highlighting groups into readable chunks.


Character
Catches a single letter — a typo, a changed suffix, a digit. The display breaks into small pieces, so it suits one clause at a time rather than a whole document.





Why filter out differences


Text copied from a word processor and text copied from a PDF carry different spacing and line breaks. Compared literally, sentences that are actually identical light up red and bury the real changes.

Frequently asked questions


Is the text I paste in sent anywhere?
No. The comparison itself runs inside your browser, so the tool works with the connection switched off. Close the page and what you pasted goes with it.


When should I use word rather than character comparison?
Word comparison reads better when whole sentences or clauses have been rewritten, which is the usual case in a redlined contract. Character comparison catches a single letter — a typo, or a changed suffix. It breaks the display into small pieces, so it suits a close look at one clause rather than a whole document.


Why would I ignore whitespace?
Text copied out of a word processor and text copied out of a PDF carry different spacing and indentation. Compared literally, sentences that are actually identical light up red across the whole document. Ignoring whitespace skips that noise and leaves the real changes visible.


Can I jump between the changes?
Previous and next buttons step through the changes one at a time, and the counter shows where you are. In side-by-side view the two panes scroll together, so the matching passage stays opposite.


How do I keep a record of the result?
Copy summary puts the removals and additions on your clipboard as paired entries. Print produces a report with the highlighting intact, which you can save as a PDF and attach for approval.


How long a document can it handle?
Ordinary contracts and reports are no trouble. Character comparison on a very long document can get slow, so scan it by word first and then pull out just the clause you need for a character-level look.
