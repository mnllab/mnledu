It cannot judge fashion. It can name the incident.

This tool sees exactly three colours: top, bottom and shoes. It knows nothing about fit, fabric, occasion, your face, or what is currently fashionable.

So the result is not advice. It deliberately over-interprets those three colours and assigns the outfit an unnecessarily specific comedy label. Good outfit, bad outfit, expensive outfit, borrowed outfit — everyone gets treated with the same mild disrespect.

First it makes a rough guess at where the outfit is

When a photo is loaded, colours that frequently appear around the outer edges are treated as likely background. Pixels that differ from those colours help form a rough outfit box. This is simple colour-and-position logic, not AI person recognition.

Use the four white handles to adjust the top, bottom, left and right edges, and the two yellow handles to divide top, bottom and shoes. Moving handles does not run another scan. Adjust as much as you like, then press Scan this area when you are ready.

50 case names

The detector still starts from broad colour situations — fluorescent, hard clash, all-black, bright neutrals, beige, pastel, double denim, nature tones, repeated reds, repeated blues, accent colour, neutrals, close tones and awkward contrast. Inside those groups, hue, saturation and lightness select from 50 different case names.

So all-black does not always return the same joke. It may become Triple Insurance On Playing It Safe, Black Has Been Given Full Authority, or The Colour Meeting Was Cancelled. The choice is deterministic from the colour values, so the same photo and crop produce the same result.

The Calm Index is not a style score. It is just a joke number for how quietly the colours coexist. A 90 is not praise and a 10 is not failure.

Background colours are filtered where possible

The scanner builds a small palette from colours repeatedly found near the image edges, then ignores similar pixels inside the selected outfit area before choosing representative colours. This helps when a wall or floor occupies a large part of the photo.

It is not perfect segmentation. Busy streets, multiple people or patterned backgrounds can still confuse it. In those cases, pulling the handles closer to the actual clothes is the quickest fix.

Even a perfectly coordinated outfit still gets teased

If the palette is calm, the tool will not simply say “Great match.” Cream-on-cream may become a human vanilla latte, all-black may be accused of taking out triple insurance on playing it safe, and one-tone dressing may become a unanimous colour meeting.

The success condition here is not accurate fashion consulting. It is someone reading the result and saying, “What on earth is this?” before laughing.

Frequently asked questions

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?
No. Photo processing and colour extraction happen in your browser. The page may load external style or script resources, but the photo you choose is not uploaded to a server by this tool.
Does moving a handle rescan the photo?
No. The handles only change the selected area. Press “Scan this area” when you actually want the colours recalculated.
Why is the verdict so ridiculous?
That is the intended behaviour. It takes three colours and invents an incident name. It is not measuring whether the outfit is actually good.
Does a high Calm Index mean I dress well?
No. It means the colours are relatively similar or subdued. A low number means they have more to argue about. Neither one is a fashion grade.
Can I use a friend’s photo?
Only with their agreement, and ideally while laughing together. This was not built to attack anyone’s appearance or clothes.

This is an entertainment tool that turns colour combinations into jokes. The verdict is not an assessment of fashion, appearance or taste. Photo processing happens in the browser; if you use somebody else’s photo, get their permission first.
