A long list makes the choice harder, not easier

You write out ten candidate titles and stare at them. They all look plausible and all look slightly wrong. Half an hour later you are no closer.

Weighing several things at once is genuinely difficult. Choosing between two is easy. This tool leans on that. Paste your candidates one per line, it pairs them off, and one is left at the end.

You do not need a reason

Trying to justify why one is better puts you straight back where you started. Just click whichever your eye goes to first. The one your finger reaches for is usually the one a reader reaches for too.

Arrow keys work as well — left is red, right is blue. Keys 1 and 2 do the same, which is much quicker on a long list.

How the bracket works

Your entries get shuffled first, so writing something down early does not give it an advantage. Each round pairs them up, and winners move on.

An odd count is fine. The unpaired entry skips that round and advances. The screen tells you so, which saves any confusion about how it survived without a match.

The number of choices is always one fewer than the number of entries. Seven entries, six decisions; twenty entries, nineteen. Eight to sixteen finishes without dragging.

You get standings too

Not just the winner — entries are placed by how far they got. Whoever lost the final takes second, the semi-final losers follow, and so on.

Knowing the runner-up is useful in its own right, since it gives you a fallback if the winner ends up not sitting well.

If you misclick

Undo steps back to the previous match, and you can keep pressing it to go further back. Backspace does the same from the keyboard.

At the end, play again reshuffles and runs it once more. A different bracket sometimes produces a different winner, which itself tells you something — anything that wins twice is worth trusting.

Not just titles

Anything that splits on line breaks works: business name candidates, ad copy, company taglines, team names, or settling on lunch.

Frequently asked questions

How many entries can I add?
Two at minimum, with no upper limit set. Bear in mind you have to make one fewer choice than the number of entries, so thirty entries means twenty-nine decisions. Somewhere between eight and sixteen finishes without dragging.
Does the count have to be even?
No. When the numbers do not pair up, one entry skips that round and advances. You are told on screen that it went through unopposed, so there is no confusion about how it survived.
Can I use the keyboard?
Left and right arrow keys pick a side, and 1 and 2 work as well. With a long list it is considerably faster than the mouse.
What if I pick the wrong one?
The undo button steps back to the previous match, and you can press it repeatedly to keep going back.
What happens with duplicate entries?
Identical lines are merged into one. Surrounding spaces and blank lines are cleaned up too, so you can paste a rough list straight in. You are told how many were merged.
Can I keep the result?
You can copy the winning line, and the full standings as well. Placings are worked out from how far each entry got, so you can also see the runner-up.

Everything runs inside your browser and nothing you type is sent to any server. Closing the page clears your progress, so copy anything you want to keep. Because the bracket is shuffled, the same entries can produce a different winner — this is a device for helping you decide, not an oracle.
