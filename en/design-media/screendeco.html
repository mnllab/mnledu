Why a raw screenshot looks unfinished

Drop a screenshot into a slide and it tends to sit awkwardly. A white capture on a white background loses its edges entirely, and the cropped boundary is left exposed.

Adding a background, some padding and a soft shadow changes the impression completely, without touching the content. This tool does that in a few clicks, and can add a Mac style window bar so the capture reads as an application rather than a fragment.

When to use it

Slides
Product screens and demo captures that need to sit properly on a slide.
Blog and social posts
Tidying a capture so the surrounding article looks considered.
Proposals and pitch decks
Work in progress screens read as more finished with a background and shadow behind them.
Documentation
Giving every screenshot in a manual the same consistent treatment.

How to use it

  1. Paste or drop in an image.
  2. Adjust the background, padding, corners and shadow.
  3. Turn on the window bar if it suits the context.
  4. Use Copy image and paste it where you need it.

Settings

Background
The colour behind the capture. Choosing something that contrasts with your document is what gives the image an edge.
Padding, corners and shadow
Padding relieves the crowding, while rounded corners and a shadow lift the capture off the page. Restraint reads better than maximum settings.
Mac window bar and traffic lights
Adds a title bar above the capture. Suited to introducing a piece of software.

Frequently asked questions

Is my image uploaded
No. Everything happens in your browser.
Can I save it as a file
Yes, though copying and pasting directly into a document is usually quicker. Download is there when you need a file.
Does it reduce the quality
The original is kept at full size and the background and padding are added around it, so there is no meaningful loss.

This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server or stored anywhere, and closing the page discards the working data.
