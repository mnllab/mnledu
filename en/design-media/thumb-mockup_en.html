When the text you agonised over sits under a duration badge

You put a line of copy in the bottom right corner of the thumbnail, publish, and find the duration badge sitting squarely on top of it. Shorts is worse: a column of like and comment icons down the right, the channel name and title across the bottom.

What looked fine filling your editor does not survive the actual app. This tool shows you that state before you publish.

What gets covered

💻 Regular video · bottom right
The duration badge is always there, covering roughly 26% across and 13% down.
💻 Regular video · along the bottom
A red progress bar appears on anything partly watched. Hover and the lower half darkens while a play button lands in the centre.
📱 Shorts · right side
Like, dislike, comment and share stack vertically, taking about 22% of the width.
📱 Shorts · bottom
Channel name, subscribe button and title cover the bottom 22% or so. This is the one most often missed.

Turn on show covered areas and those regions appear hatched. Keep your text and faces outside them.

Where the title gets cut

Titles show two lines at most in every layout, and anything beyond that is truncated. The catch is that the available width differs between layouts, so a title that fits neatly on desktop can still be cut on Shorts.

This tool does not estimate from character counts. It measures the height the browser actually rendered, so the font and letter spacing are accounted for. If any layout truncates, you are told which.

In search results only the first 40 characters or so really register, so lead with the words that matter.

How to use it

  1. Load your thumbnail — drag it in or paste with Ctrl+V.
  2. Type the title and watch how it wraps.
  3. Switch between desktop, mobile and Shorts, or view all three side by side.
  4. Turn on hover state and partly watched to see the worst case.
  5. Save the preview if you want to compare drafts later.

Dimensions

Regular videos use 16:9, commonly 1280×720 pixels. Shorts are 9:16 portrait. Load something with a different ratio and you are told which edges would be cropped.

Frequently asked questions

Is my thumbnail uploaded anywhere?
No. Reading the image and laying the mockup over it both happen inside your browser, so the tool works with the connection switched off. Closing the page clears the image with it.
Which parts get covered?
On a regular video the duration badge always sits bottom right, and a progress bar appears along the bottom on hover or when you have watched part of it. On Shorts the icon column on the right and the channel block at the bottom take a substantial share of the frame, so text placed there will not be read.
How is title truncation determined?
Not by counting characters, but by measuring the height the browser actually rendered. Anything past two lines is truncated with an ellipsis, and you are told which of the layouts it happens in.
What does the covered-area overlay show?
It hatches the regions the interface sits on top of. Leave it on while reviewing and you can see at a glance where the safe area ends. Keep text and faces outside those regions and nothing gets hidden.
What dimensions should I use?
Regular videos use 16 by 9, commonly 1280 by 720 pixels. Shorts are 9 by 16 portrait. Load an image with a different ratio and you are told which edges would be cropped.
Can I save the result?
The preview can be saved as an image, which is handy for sharing with a team or comparing drafts. The saved image includes the interface mockup.
