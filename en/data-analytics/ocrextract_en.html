Retyping text out of a picture

A screenshot where copying is disabled. A photograph of a form. A scanned document. Getting the words out means typing them again, and even a small table takes a surprising amount of time.

OCR finds the characters in an image and converts them to text. This tool does that inside your browser, so the image is never uploaded. That matters for the things people usually need to transcribe: contracts, identity documents, invoices.

When to use it

Text from a screenshot
When a page blocks selection, or the content is an image rather than text.
Scanned documents
Pulling the contents out of a paper form that was scanned or photographed.
Conference slides
Turning the photos you took of slides into notes you can search.
Receipts and statements
Getting figures off a photographed document and into a spreadsheet.

How to use it

  1. Add an image.
  2. Choose the recognition language. English, English with Korean, or Korean only.
  3. Correct anything that came out wrong directly in the result box.
  4. Use Copy all recognised text.

Settings

Recognition language
Narrowing the language improves accuracy. If the image is English only, choose English rather than a combined mode.
Tidy line breaks
Reflows the recognised text into paragraphs instead of the line by line output.
Run again
Re-runs recognition after you change the language or swap the image.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get better results
Large, sharp characters help most. Crop and straighten a tilted photo, and retake anything with heavy shadow or glare. Handwriting is generally not recognised well.
Is my image uploaded
No. Recognition runs in your browser and the image never leaves your device.
Are tables preserved
The text is extracted but the row and column structure is not. You will need to rebuild the table after copying.
Why is the first run slow
The recognition model is downloaded on first use. After that it is cached and subsequent runs start straight away.

This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server or stored anywhere, and closing the page discards the working data.
