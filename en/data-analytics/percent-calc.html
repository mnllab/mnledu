Where margin maths usually goes wrong

Add 30% to a $10 cost and you get $13. That is 30% of cost. A 30% margin on the selling price is about $14.30. Which base you use changes the answer, and the two sides of a negotiation often mean different things by the same number.

Then platform fees, advertising, shipping and tax come off the top. This tool works through those in order, so you can see the price to list and what actually reaches your account.

Checking what you keep

How to use it

  1. Enter your unit cost and quantity.
  2. Set the target margin, platform fee, advertising share and shipping.
  3. Choose your sales tax or VAT setting, or enter a rate directly.
  4. Read off the price to list and what lands in your account.

Settings

Sales tax / VAT
Presets cover the common cases: the United States at 0%, since sales tax is normally added at checkout rather than built into the listed price, plus Canada, Singapore, Korea, Japan, Australia, Germany, the United Kingdom, France and Italy. Enter a rate handles anything else, including a state or provincial rate you want folded into the price.
Target margin (share of price)
Margin measured against the selling price, which is the convention in retail. If your supplier quotes markup on cost instead, the two numbers will not match.
Advertising (share of revenue)
Enter advertising as a percentage of revenue and it is deducted before the margin figure.
Move to the tax account
Shows the portion of revenue that belongs to the tax authority rather than to you. Setting it aside as it arrives avoids a shortfall at filing time.

Frequently asked questions

Margin on cost or on price
Use whichever your buyer, supplier or platform uses. Retail generally quotes margin on the selling price, while manufacturing often quotes markup on cost. The important thing is that both sides mean the same thing.
Why is the United States set to 0%
In most US states sales tax is added at checkout rather than included in the advertised price, so the listed price carries no tax. If you do want a rate folded in, choose Enter a rate and type your combined state and local rate.
How is tax calculated from a gross amount
When tax is already inside the amount, the tax portion is the gross multiplied by rate ÷ (1 + rate). At 10% that is one eleventh, which is why a $110 sale contains $10 of tax rather than $11.
Can I rely on this for filing
Treat it as an estimate for planning. Registration status, thresholds, exemptions and reduced rates all vary by country and often by state or province. Check with an accountant before filing.
