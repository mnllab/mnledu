Two calculations people get wrong by eyeballing them

"What will this be worth in 20 years" doesn't come from multiplying a monthly amount by time. Compounding accelerates as it goes, which is hard to track by hand. Averaging down has the same problem in reverse — lowering your average price feels like progress, but it's easy to skip the step of working out how far the price still needs to rise to break even.

This tool shows both calculations on one screen. Type a number and the chart and results update immediately, with no button to press.

When to use it

Planning for retirement
Seeing what your current contribution becomes in N years, and what that is actually worth once inflation is factored in.
Tuning your contribution
Adjusting the monthly amount to see how much sooner a target could be reached.
Deciding whether to average down
Checking what your average price becomes before committing to an additional purchase, and how far it still has to rise to break even.
Setting a sell target
Seeing the exact price needed for a given return, laid out as a table.

How to use it

  1. Choose FIRE Compound Simulator or Cost Averaging Calculator at the top.
  2. Type numbers into the fields on the left. Thousands separators are added automatically.
  3. The chart and results on the right update as you type.

Settings

Annual return and inflation rate
Assumed constant for the whole period, which simplifies real markets that move year to year. Treat the result as a reference point, not a forecast.
Target return
In the averaging calculator, enter the return you want and the matching sell price appears in the table.

Frequently asked questions

Could the real result differ from this
Yes. Return and inflation are simplified to constant values for the whole period, while real markets fluctuate year to year. Treat this as a reference and talk to a professional before acting on it.
Is anything I enter sent anywhere
No. Everything is calculated inside this browser and nothing is sent to a server.
What price does the averaging calculator use as the baseline
The rise needed for break-even and for your target return is calculated from the additional purchase price, treated as today's market price.

This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server or stored anywhere, and closing the page discards the working data.
